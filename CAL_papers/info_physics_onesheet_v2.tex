\textbf{Origin.} \ CAL is not a theory of quantum gravity. It is an information-physics engine for non-Markovian reality, built to lift self-attention to a field-theoretic form from first principles of cognition, causality and intelligence. Its derivations of known physics are unit tests: a law of causal memory that cannot recover established physics has no business modeling minds or societies.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Definition 1 (Information).} \ Information $:=$ distinguishability of physical states.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Remark 1 (Difference and observation).} \ To distinguish physical states is to observe a difference between them. Change is a difference between states along an ordered evolution; thus change is temporally ordered distinguishability. Observation here denotes physical distinguishability, not a required conscious observer.\\[2pt]
\textbf{Definition 2 (Causality).} \ Causality $:=$ change in physical state, including its rate of change.\\[2pt]
\textbf{The law.} \ $T(x)=\alpha J(x)-(1-\alpha)\int_{J^-(x)}k(x,x')\,U(x,x')\,J(x')\,d^4x'$, with $J=\sum_a Z_ae_a$, $Z_a=A_ae^{-H_a/\tau_E}+B_ae^{iS_a/\hbar_{\rm eff}}$, $A_a+B_a=1$; $k$ is the memory kernel and $U$ the spacetime and internal transports (On Information Physics, Eq.~61, scalar-abbreviated).

\step{1}{History is more than its endpoints}
\say{The fundamental theorem of calculus recovers net change. A history functional can tell apart two trajectories with the same endpoints.}
\[
X(t)-X(t_0)=\int_{t_0}^{t}\dot X\,d\tau,\qquad M(t)=\int_{t_0}^{t}K(t,\tau)\,F\big(X(\tau)\big)\,d\tau .
\]
\say{Only two kernels make the present sufficient: the Dirac limit (forgetful) and the light-cone limit (faithful). Because $K$ depends on the evaluation time, $M$ is not additive under concatenation, so it is not a functor to any additive group; its carrier is the path category $\mathrm{Path}(S)$, outside the de Rham tiers.}

\step{2}{Two weights in one channel}
\say{Each channel holds a Boltzmann weight and a Feynman weight. The substitution $1/k_BT\to1/(i\hbar)$ turns one into the other, the move that makes the path integral a partition function with quantropy as its entropy.}
\[
Z_a=A_a e^{-H_a/\tau_E}+B_a e^{iS_a/\hbar_{\rm eff}},\qquad J=\textstyle\sum_{a=0}^{3}Z_a e_a\in M_2(\Cp).
\]

\step{3}{The Born rule from positive closure}
\say{Closing the carrier with its own adjoint gives a positive matrix. Its trace adds up the channels; positive effects read probabilities off it.}
\[
\varrho=JJ^\dagger\succeq0,\quad \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\varrho={\textstyle\sum_a}|Z_a|^2,\quad p_m=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(E_m JJ^\dagger)}{\operatorname{tr}(JJ^\dagger)} .
\]
\say{For $J=\psi\eta^\dagger$ and $E_m=\phi_m\phi_m^\dagger$ this is $p_m=|\phi_m^\dagger\psi|^2/\psi^\dagger\psi$. The inputs are positive effects and trace evaluation; the squared amplitude is algebraic.}

\step{4}{Lorentz from the determinant}
\say{The same matrix has a real Hermitian slice. Its determinant is the Minkowski interval, and $SL(2,\Cp)$ congruence preserves it.}
\[
X=x^\mu\sigma_\mu,\quad \det X=(x^0)^2-|\vec x|^2,\quad \det(GXG^\dagger)=\det X .
\]
\say{The closure sits in the future cone: $\det\varrho=|\det J|^2\ge0$, rank one is null, rank two is timelike. This is the $\Cp$ case of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb K)\cong\mathfrak{so}(\dim\mathbb K+1,1)$.}

\step{5}{The Bloch ball is a slice of the cone}
\say{Normalize the trace to one and the future cone's cross-section is the Bloch ball.}
\[
\hat\varrho=\frac{\varrho}{\operatorname{tr}\varrho}=\tfrac12\big(I_2+\vec r\cdot\vec\sigma\big),\quad |\vec r|\le1,\quad \det\hat\varrho=\tfrac14\big(1-|\vec r|^2\big).
\]
\say{Surface: pure, null closures. Interior: mixed, timelike closures. Rotations keep the normalization; boosts do not, so the Bloch section is a normalization, not a Lorentz-invariant slice. \textbf{[Derived]} $2\sqrt{\det\varrho}/\operatorname{tr}\varrho=\sqrt{1-|\vec r|^2}=1/\gamma=\sqrt{2S_L}$, the concurrence of a purification ($S_L=1-\operatorname{tr}\hat\varrho^2$); pure closures are null.}

\step{6}{The Killing form on the generator slice}
\say{On traceless anti-Hermitian generators the determinant is the Killing form of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, up to scale.}
\[
\det A=|\vec y|^2=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}A^2=-\tfrac18\,\kappa(A,A),\qquad A=y^je_j .
\]
\say{On all of $\mathfrak u(2)$ the Killing form misses the centre; the determinant restores it with opposite sign, $\det A=-(y^0)^2+|\vec y|^2$. Trace gives probability, determinant gives the metric, Killing gives the internal symmetry: three readings of one carrier.}

\step{7}{Real slices of one complex form}
\say{Over $\Cp$, $\det J=Z_0^2+Z_1^2+Z_2^2+Z_3^2$ has no signature. Choosing which channels carry an $i$ chooses the slice.}
{\footnotesize
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\mathbb H & (+,+,+,+) & \text{quaternion norm}\\
i\mathfrak u(2)\ \text{(Hermitian)} & (+,-,-,-) & \text{closures, Born, Minkowski}\\
\mathfrak u(2)\ \text{(anti-Hermitian)} & (-,+,+,+) & \text{transport generators}
\end{array}
\]}
\say{Minkowski is the Euclidean form with the three vector channels rotated by $i$: the algebraic quarter turn of Wick rotation. \textbf{[Derived]} Multiplication by $i$ swaps the state and generator slices isometrically. The two pairings use two bars: Born uses ${}^\dagger$; the Lorentz pairing applies the bar $X\mapsto\operatorname{adj}X=\epsilon X^{T}\epsilon^{T}$ ($\epsilon=i\sigma_2$) once, $X\operatorname{adj}X=\det X\,I_2$; applying it twice returns $X$, giving back the Born pairing on the Hermitian slice.}

\step{8}{Order is kept, but lossily}
\say{Left-acting histories are remembered by $JJ^\dagger$ and erased by $J^\dagger J$.}
\[
J_{tx}J_{tx}^\dagger=U_tU_x\varrho U_x^\dagger U_t^\dagger\ \neq\ J_{xt}J_{xt}^\dagger,\qquad J_{tx}^\dagger J_{tx}=J_{xt}^\dagger J_{xt}=J^\dagger J .
\]
\say{The closure distinguishes some histories and identifies others: neither faithful nor forgetful. The commutator of two closures is anti-Hermitian, $[\varrho_1,\varrho_2]^\dagger=-[\varrho_1,\varrho_2]$ \textbf{[Derived]}, so order-sensitive content lives on the generator slice; the Jarlskog CP invariant $\det[H_u,H_d]$ is of this form \textbf{[Proposed]}.}

\step{9}{Fractional accumulation keeps both readouts}
\say{The Weyl integral rescales the decay sector and rotates the phase sector. The rotation between them is the Wick angle.}
\[
I^\alpha e^{st}=s^{-\alpha}e^{st},\quad I^\alpha Z_a=A_a\lambda_a^{-\alpha}e^{\lambda_a t}+B_a(i\omega_a)^{-\alpha}e^{i\omega_a t},\quad \theta=\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}.
\]
\say{$\theta$ is a quarter turn at $\alpha=1$ and a half turn at $\alpha=2$. Since $\varrho^{(\alpha)}=(I^\alpha J)(I^\alpha J)^\dagger\succeq0$, the Born and cone identities hold at every order.}

\step{10}{Energy conditions from positivity}
\say{Build a rank-two tensor from closure vectors. Positive weights on future-causal vectors give the energy conditions with no extra input.}
\[
\begin{gathered}
T^{\mu\nu}={\textstyle\sum_i}w_i x_i^\mu x_i^\nu,\qquad T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu={\textstyle\sum_i}w_i(x_i\!\cdot u)^2\ge0,\\
T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu={\textstyle\sum_i}w_i(x_i\!\cdot u)\,x_i^\mu,\qquad T^\mu{}_\mu={\textstyle\sum_i}w_i\det\varrho_i .
\end{gathered}
\]
\say{In the accumulation the weights are $\alpha_{\rm frac}$ (present) and $(\alpha_{\rm frac}-1)k$ (history). With $k\ge0$: $\alpha_{\rm frac}\ge1$ gives weak, null, and dominant energy conditions automatically.}

\step{11}{A weight gradient acts as friction \ \status{Derived}}
\say{Weight the action by a positive function and the Euler--Lagrange equations gain a term set by the log-gradient of the weight.}
\[
\partial_\mu\pi^\mu-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Phi}=-(\partial_\mu\ln W)\,\pi^\mu .
\]
\say{$W=e^{\gamma t}$ gives the Caldirola--Kanai damped oscillator; $W=a^3$ gives Hubble friction. Exchanging the two integrals of the CAL action gives exactly this form, with $W(x)=\alpha_{\rm frac}+(\alpha_{\rm frac}-1)\int_{J^+(x)}k\,d^4z$ \textbf{[Derived; finiteness of $W$ open]}: uniform accumulation is invisible, a change in accumulation acts as friction, and matter exchanges energy with the weight, $\nabla^\mu(WT_{\mu\nu})=L_m\nabla_\nu W$.}

\step{12}{Why closed systems forget \ \status{Proposed}}
\say{Finite capacity forces erasure at $k_BT\ln2$ per bit, so a closed system reaches only the forgetful limit: physical memory is lossy.}
